# Taste standard

What a good client site is, from the user's approvals and rejections. It says what the site must achieve, not how it
looks: idea, layout, type, colour, medium and motion are the builder's own exploration. Where it sits among the kit's
rules: "Which rule wins" in `AGENTS.md`.

## The bar
- **Approve on sight, beside the best.** Every hero is judged next to two sites from its category ("The bar" in
  `reference-library.md`) and the design lab. Would the owner stop scrolling? Would the brand's own marketing team
  publish it? Does it look like this business and no other?
- **What approved heroes share:** a format the customer already understands, unexpected for the category, that matches
  what the customer does here; built from the client's real photos, products, people, places and prices; a picture you
  remember tomorrow with the words hidden; it says what the business is in seconds and gives one clear action.

## The idea
- **The client's thing becomes the interface.** Start from what the customer does here, or an object from the client's
  own trade, and let the visitor use it: a run-of-show cue sheet for an emcee, a lyrics player for a live-music bar, a
  cut-open building for a contractor, a bento box that fills one slot per scroll for a lunch shop, a tower where you
  pick a floor. Those are taken: find this client's own. Never a metaphor (a sundial for perfume, hours for scents) and never a generic app screen, dashboard or
  admin panel.
- **The builder owns the idea.** It writes three different hero ideas in one line each and builds the strongest for
  this client and customer, not the safest. Each variant has its own idea, layout, type and palette.
- **The tone fits what is sold and who buys:** luxury looks luxury, corporate looks corporate, a bar at night looks like
  a bar at night (`site-types.md`). The tone sets how an idea is finished, not which formats are allowed. Variants 1-3
  keep the business's tone; variant 4 is free in tone and medium too.

## The material
- **Real material leads.** The client's photos, films, products, people and places, prepared before design: cropped
  clean, one grade across the site, upscaled only while sharp, products cut out where the edge is clean, short film
  loops cut from their clips. Licensed stock of the real subject is fine, labelled "Photo for illustration".
  Generated images and video from free tools (never Higgsfield) for art, backgrounds and camera moves, never passed
  off as the client's work, product, people or place.
- **3D is welcome, at photo quality.** A 3D product or place may lead the hero when it looks as good as a product
  photo: a real model (the client's file, a licensed model, or image-to-3D from the client's photo, cleaned up),
  real materials, HDRI lighting, a soft contact shadow, set beside the real photo to compare. A product built from code
  primitives that looks cheap fails; use the photo instead.
- **Invent nothing:** no prices, claims, reviews, names or numbers. Unknowns show as `[client to confirm]`.

## Motion
- **It moves, and the motion means something:** a short opening that never blocks, something alive at rest, and a
  scroll move that runs smoothly both ways (at most one screen of pinning in the hero). One signature moment per page,
  made from the client's thing; every section has motion with a job; joins between sections are designed.
- **The craft layer every variant has:** smooth scroll, headlines that rise from a mask, images that reveal or move
  with depth, page changes without a white flash. Tools and numbers: `motion.md`, `craft-numbers.md`.
- **Judge it moving.** Stills hide cheap motion: check with `hero-frames.cjs --motion` (the opening, mid-scroll and
  hover states).

## Will they buy?
- **The buyer's path sets the order:** What is it? Is it for me? Is it real? How much? How do I get it?
- **Basics in every variant:** real people, products or premises up front; a price or "from" price and the main action
  within about 1.5 screens; proof before the ask; the action always in reach on a phone; every product, price, page,
  proof and way to order the client has. Branding sites may hold prices until after the story.
- **One tool that fits the business,** answering a real customer question and ending in a filled-in WhatsApp message.
- **Inner pages as good as the home page.** Words short, specific and in the customer's language.

## Rejected on sight
- **Copying a reference:** its images, logo, copy or colours, or a page that could pass for it with the logo hidden.
- **Variants that look alike,** or at different levels of completeness.
- **Too boxy:** most of the page is rows of cards on a flat background.
- **Messy layering:** words over busy images, accidental overlaps, things under the sticky header.
- **Bad images:** cut-off products, odd ratios, stretched or blurry photos, cheap-looking 3D.
- **Bad motion:** short clips rewinding or looping to fill time, jitter, scroll lag, a heavy first load.
- **Body text left half faded** by scroll position. A fill that means something (karaoke lyrics, progress) is fine.
- **Browser-default controls:** a white date popup, number spinner arrows, an OS dropdown.
- **Small breakages:** sideways scroll, white gaps at the edges, cards changing height and shifting the page.
- **Red with gold and brush calligraphy** for a Chinese client that isn't festive: it reads as New Year.

## Type
Fonts with character that fit the client, the display face set big enough to carry the brand. Not these, unless the
client's brand uses them, because they read as AI defaults: Inter, Space Grotesk, Playfair Display, Fraunces, DM Sans,
Manrope, Syne, Outfit.

## Colour
From the brand and the client's world, never from a metaphor. Variants 3 and 4 may try a mood pair with one clashing
accent (`colour.md`).
