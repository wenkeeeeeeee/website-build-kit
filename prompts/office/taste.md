# Taste standard

Taken from the user's own feedback across past builds, and from the sites they were happy with. It decides what
counts as good. When a brief and this file disagree, the brief wins only if the user said so.

## Rejected on sight
- **Variants that look alike.** The same template in different colours is the most repeated complaint. Shared headers
  count, and so do shared hero layouts and section orders.
- **A good idea with no context.** Strong transitions and a clever concept still fail if the page doesn't sound like it
  knows the business. A site for a skin condition must talk about the itch, the flare-ups, the conditions it treats,
  why they come back, how the cream works, real customers' results and what it costs. The specific content is the point.
- **Basic and plain.** Sections with a heading, a paragraph and a picture. Empty-looking panels. A plain footer. Pages
  whose only motion is fade-ins.
- **Messy layering.** Words over busy images, elements overlapping by accident, things sliding under or over the
  sticky header. Overlap must look deliberate.
- **Sections with no clear purpose.** If you can't say what a section is delivering, cut or rebuild it.
- **Inner pages weaker than the home page.** Every tab gets the same components and motion as the home page.
- **Missing client content.** If the client has a catalogue, recipes, gifts or awards, the site has them too.
- **Variants at different levels of completeness.** Every variant carries the whole business. They differ in design and
  story, never in how much of the client they cover, so one must not be a full site while another is a thin concept.
- **A changed logo.** Use the original. Don't redraw, outline or restyle it.
- **Invented words.** No slogan, claim or theme that isn't in the client's own material.
- **"Branding" built as a shop.** When a client wants branding, they mean storytelling: the whole home page is one
  story with transitions between products. No cards, prices or adverts until a quiet shop link at the end.
- **Bad images.** Cut-off food or products, odd ratios, circles cut from square photos, low-res pictures stretched
  full-screen, and slow zooms on blurry stills.
- **Bad motion.** Short clips rewinding or looping to fill time, jitter, scroll lag, a heavy first load.
- **Small breakages.** White gaps at corners or edges, sections that don't fit one screen, cards that change height
  and shift the page when an arrow is clicked.

## What the approved sites do
These sites, for an optician and a self-photo studio, are the level to reach.
- **The hero is the client's own object doing something.** The logo's O opens into the campaign film. A frame comes
  apart in 3D, part by part, with labels. A gold frame assembles on the brand colour. A photo strip prints out of the
  page. Every room is a magazine cover you turn by scrolling.
- **The client's own object runs through the whole site, not only the hero.** It is the intro, the transitions between
  sections, the image frames and the small details. For a balloon stylist, that was an intro of balloons flying up the
  screen, balloons carrying each section change, and photos framed inside balloons. Tasteful variants without enough of
  the client's world read as good but not theirs, so use more of it than feels safe, at the same level of craft.
- **One signature moment, then sections that keep moving.** One long scroll set-piece (3–5 screens of pinning), then
  sections that each fit one screen, each with its own motion graphic. Whole home pages run about 8–19 screens. Clients
  called variants with too little of this basic and plain, so meet `motion.md`.
- **A tool that fits the business.** A lens tester, a face-shape finder, a room dial, a venue dial. Each answers a
  question customers really ask, and each ends in a WhatsApp message.
- **Plumbing in the business's language.** A "try-on list" or "shoot list" instead of a basket.
- **Giant type doing a job.** The product's name outlined behind the product. A complete wordmark closing the footer,
  never cut off.
- **Each variant is its own world.** A different type pair, palette, header and section order, with fonts chosen as
  the Type section says.
- **Real proof beside the mood.** Real photos of rooms, staff and equipment show the business exists. Campaign images
  carry the mood, and are labelled as illustration.
- **Sites that know the customer.** A strong manually built skincare site opened on the customer's own question
  ("Itchy skin that keeps coming back?"), then gave a "Which one sounds like you?" list of named conditions, why it keeps
  happening, the three-step answer, a side-by-side with other cream types (no brands), a real before-and-after slider
  with named customers, how to use it, packages with was and now prices, plain answers to common questions, and where
  to buy or meet the team.
- **Products as loud full-screen worlds.** A wall where each product takes over the screen in its own colour, with its
  real name, facts and price, was picked out as very good in a pitch. So was a dark rail stepping through the real menu,
  with dishes you can add to a list.
- **Local detail stays.** The client's own languages, real branch names, live open or closed status, and honest
  `[client to confirm]` tags instead of guesses.

## Type
Fonts are the fastest way a site reads as machine-made. Builds kept landing on the same few faces, so fonts now come
from the client's world.
- **Not these**, unless the client's own brand uses them: Inter, Inter Tight, Fraunces, Figtree, DM Sans, DM Serif,
  DM Mono, Instrument Serif, Instrument Sans, Bricolage Grotesque, Manrope, Sora, Syne, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans,
  Space Grotesk, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Newsreader, Archivo, Bodoni Moda, JetBrains Mono.
- **Start from what is really there.** Look at the client's signage, packaging, menu and posters, and any fonts on
  their site. Find a face with the same character, so a gig poster, a shop sign and a pharmacy label each lead somewhere
  different. Then check what the references really use, and find your own free face that does the same job.
- **Look past the popular lists.** Try Fontshare, Velvetyne, Open Foundry, Collletttivo and the less-used end of Google
  Fonts. The licence must allow commercial web use. Self-host the `woff2` files in the site.
- **One face with a point of view, one quiet face to read.** Two safe faces look like a template. If the pair would
  suit any other client, pick again.
- **Check every language.** The display face must carry the client's languages and punctuation. Noto is only for a
  script with no better option.
- **Other tells to avoid:** small uppercase mono labels above every heading, "01 / 02 / 03" section numbers with no
  meaning, one italic word in a headline, cream paper with a serif by default, gradient text and glass cards.

## Media
Build with what exists. Ask for images or clips only when they lift the idea, and give ready-to-paste prompts with no
text or audio, as few and as short as possible to save credits. If an idea looks bad with stills, clips won't save it.
