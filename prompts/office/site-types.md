# Site types

Research picks one before choosing references. The type decides the first impression.

## Shop
- **Choose when:** many products with prices, an active marketplace shop, customers who already know what they want.
- **Home page:** shows the range fast, then proves quality. One signature moment built from the products.
- **Pages:** home, catalogue with filters, product, about, where to buy, contact or quote.
- **Plumbing:** the full starter; the basket ends in WhatsApp or the marketplace.
- **If it solves a problem** (skin, health, pain, hair, cleaning), it sells the answer, not the product. Home page order:
  the customer's problem in their words, the needs it covers, why it keeps happening, how this works, real results, how
  it compares, how to use it, packages, questions, where to buy. No medical promises the client can't back; keep
  "individual results vary" wording.

## Branding
- **Choose when:** the product is premium, heritage or hard to judge in a list; the client says "rebrand" or "story"; or
  their pages show pride in origin and craft rather than prices.
- **Home page:** one story, transitions carrying you from product to product. Prices and shop links come after the
  story; the main action stays reachable in the header throughout.
- **Pages:** home as the story, brand or origin, collection, recipes or rituals, contact. The shop is secondary.
- **Plumbing:** a light catalogue and enquiry. The basket hidden until the end.

## Booking
- **Choose when:** people visit in person: restaurants, cafés, studios, clinics, salons, opticians.
- **Home page:** sells the place and the visit, then booking or finding a branch is one tap away.
- **Pages:** home, menu or services, item page, about, locations, booking, events or groups.
- **Plumbing:** rename the basket (pre-order, shoot list, try-on list). Booking and groups go to WhatsApp. A branch map
  or finder if there are several locations.

## Services
- **Choose when:** the business sells expertise or case work: consultants, legal, B2B suppliers, support services.
- **Home page:** builds trust and explains the process, then one clear enquiry.
- **Pages:** home, services, process, proof or cases, about, enquiry.
- **Plumbing:** no shop parts. Keep the quote form, or a confidential request for sensitive services.

Every type: one concept per variant, and the checks in `taste.md`.

## Tone
Research decides one tone for the client before choosing references and writes it in `PITCH.md` with the signals that
decided it. V1–V3 use it and differ in idea and layout. The free variant (V4) may leave it.

- **Playful:** the business is about joy, treats, celebration, play or personality; customers come for fun or a feeling,
  whatever the price. Snacks, sweets, drinks, desserts, gifts, balloons and parties, toys and kids' things, pets, casual
  cafés and bars, playful fashion or lifestyle brands. **Looks like:** loud colour, bold type, stickers and cut-outs,
  bouncy motion, a hero action to play with, optional sound, copy with the brand's humour.
- **Modern and professional:** the business is about trust, performance or expertise; customers come for a result they
  must rely on: health, money, safety, learning, a business need, technology that has to work. Vehicles and technology,
  clinics and health, finance and law, education, B2B, property, services, group or corporate sites. **Looks like:** a
  calm, confident layout, clean type, precise motion that explains the product, real specs and proof up front; how
  light or dark, how large the type and how dense the page come from the business's character, not this label. No games, stickers, sound or jokes in headlines.
- **Luxury and heritage** (fine dining, jewellery, watches, boutique hotels, heritage brands): the modern and
  professional tone with richer imagery, a slower pace and more space.

**How to decide.** Not by price: a cheap clinic visit is professional, an expensive party playful. Ask what the business
is about and what customers come for, then check how the client talks in its own posts and how the category leaders
look. Mixed signals: modern and professional. A professional client with a playful voice can have one warmer variant,
still clean and credible.

**Character: the tone fitted to this business.** The tone is a family, not a look: two businesses in the same tone can
need opposite sites. So research writes this business's own character in 3–5 words, worked out fresh from how it looks
and talks, what it sells, who buys and how its category leaders feel. Never reuse words from another client. Every
builder is briefed with them and decides for itself how they show in layout, type, colour, images and motion.

**Tone decides the references.** Pick Benchmarks tagged with the tone, and put the tone's words in Awwwards searches:
playful, colourful, illustrated; or premium, minimal, technology, corporate. A reference in the other tone can't lead a
variant, however good.

## Design families
Starting hints for categories that sell the same way. A client that fits none takes the family matching how its page
must sell. Each family says what the hero shows and what comes below it, never how to design it: layout, type and
staging are the builder's, and the concept in `PITCH.md` wins. They don't bind the free variant.

- **One beautiful object:** bags, eyewear, watches, jewellery, shoes, perfume, audio, furniture. **Hero shows:** the real
  product, big and sharp. **Below:** close-ups of materials and craft, each detail named.
- **Vehicles and mobility:** e-scooters, e-bikes, motorcycles, cars, EV charging, rental and sharing. Everyday technology,
  so precise and premium, never loud. **Hero shows:** the real vehicle, or a real rider using it. **Below:** a model
  switcher, one fact line (weight, range, price a month), a feature index over close-ups, spec counters, "which model
  fits you", running cost against petrol. Never a game, a cartoon road, a dashboard or a sale poster. Category brands to
  open: cowboy.com, atherenergy.com, gogoro.com, unagiscooters.com.
- **Packs and jars:** snacks, drinks, sauces, coffee, sweets, pet food. Loud colour per product, flavours and ranges.
  **Hero shows:** the product, whole and appetising. **Below:** the basket, then each flavour in its own colour, with
  the brand's humour in small copy.
- **Solves a problem:** skincare, hair, supplements, dental, pain relief, cleaning. **Hero shows:** what the product
  does, under a headline in the customer's words. **Below, right away:** how it works and whether it's safe
  (ingredients, tests, who can use it); then the problem clearing, real before-and-after and simple diagrams, not
  paragraphs. Every claim, timeline and safety line comes from the client's material or is marked `[client to confirm]`.
- **Body and movement:** physiotherapy, pilates, yoga, gyms, sports. **Hero shows:** the body in motion, from the
  client's real footage or photos, never a calm empty studio.
- **A place to visit:** restaurants, cafés, bars, hotels, spas, salons, gyms, clinics, photo studios. **Hero shows:** the
  real room or dish, sharp and whole, and one line saying what, where and when next to the booking action. **Below:**
  the menu or services, the visit, groups and events.
- **The person is the product:** emcees and hosts, event coordinators, DJs, musicians, speakers, coaches, consultants
  and personal brands. Customers book the person. **Hero shows:** the person, big and clear. **Below:** event photos
  and clips as proof, the services and how to book.
- **Work worth showing:** event styling, florists, weddings, interiors, architecture, renovation, fit-out,
  design-and-build and contractors, photography, tattoo, set design. The portfolio is the proof. **Hero shows:** the
  finished work, big. **Below:** job names, sized to the character, whose hover opens the work, and one line or thread
  carrying you through the page. For spaces (interiors, architecture, renovation, fit-out): generous space around the
  work, photos shown in their own light, never dimmed to carry text, and the trade's own drawing language as the detail
  layer (floor plans, dimension lines, drawing numbers and title blocks, "Detail 1" callouts, "not to scale" notes).
- **Guides, media and creators:** city guides, places-to-go and review accounts, listings and directories, event
  calendars, creator channels and communities. They sell a visit or outing, not a product. **Hero shows:** the best
  places or episodes as real photos or footage, with one action such as "see all spots". **Below:** filters by area,
  type and price, a map, the latest posts, how they visit and film. Category brands to open: timeout.com,
  theinfatuation.com, atlasobscura.com, airbnb.com/s/experiences.
- **Trust and expertise:** finance, law, accounting, insurance, consultants, B2B suppliers. Calm, clear, credible.
  **Hero shows:** what the client really delivers, in real photos or footage: the product, or the people at work and the
  customer's result (the finished building, the case won, the money saved). **Below:** a slider or calculator answering
  the money question early. No sound, games, props or rituals; plain words; slower motion.
- **Learning and community:** courses, coaching, academies, mentorships, schools. **Hero shows:** the teacher and real
  students at work, or a real result they reached, with the promise in one line. **Below:** a first step you can try;
  proof stays labelled and risk wording stays.
- **Origin and craft:** farms, wineries, distilleries, makers, heritage brands. **Hero shows:** the finished product,
  big. **Below:** real photos of the place and the making, and a route from the source to the product, each stop
  opening a chapter.
