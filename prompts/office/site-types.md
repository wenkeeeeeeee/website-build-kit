# Site types

Research picks one before choosing references. The type decides the first impression.

## Shop
- **Choose when:** many products with prices, an active marketplace shop, customers who already know what they want.
- **Home page:** shows the range fast, then proves quality. One signature moment built from the products.
- **Pages:** home, catalogue with filters, product, about, where to buy, contact or quote.
- **Plumbing:** the full starter; the basket ends in WhatsApp or the marketplace.
- **If it solves a problem** (skin, health, pain, hair, cleaning), it sells the answer, not the product. Home page order:
  the customer's problem in their words, the needs it covers, why it keeps happening, how this works, real results, how
  it compares, how to use it, packages, questions, where to buy. No medical promises the client can't back; keep
  "individual results vary" wording.

## Branding
- **Choose when:** the product is premium, heritage or hard to judge in a list; the client says "rebrand" or "story"; or
  their pages show pride in origin and craft rather than prices.
- **Home page:** one story, transitions carrying you from product to product. Prices and shop links come after the
  story; the main action stays reachable in the header throughout.
- **Pages:** home as the story, brand or origin, collection, recipes or rituals, contact. The shop is secondary.
- **Plumbing:** a light catalogue and enquiry. The basket hidden until the end.

## Booking
- **Choose when:** people visit in person: restaurants, cafés, studios, clinics, salons, opticians.
- **Home page:** sells the place and the visit, then booking or finding a branch is one tap away.
- **Pages:** home, menu or services, item page, about, locations, booking, events or groups.
- **Plumbing:** rename the basket (pre-order, shoot list, try-on list). Booking and groups go to WhatsApp. A branch map
  or finder if there are several locations.

## Services
- **Choose when:** the business sells expertise or case work: consultants, legal, B2B suppliers, support services.
- **Home page:** builds trust and explains the process, then one clear enquiry.
- **Pages:** home, services, process, proof or cases, about, enquiry.
- **Plumbing:** no shop parts. Keep the quote form, or a confidential request for sensitive services.

Every type: one concept per variant, and the checks in `taste.md`.

## Direction: from the category's best, not from a list
Research sets the direction for every variant before choosing references, and writes it in `PITCH.md`. It comes from
looking at real sites, never from matching the client to a list, so it works for a business the kit has never seen.
Every variant uses it, the free variant included; variants differ in idea and layout, never in direction or level.

1. **The target.** Who should this client be seen beside? The user's own words in `LEAD.md` come first ("luxury",
   "premium", "like Aesop"). Otherwise, the best-known brands in the client's exact category, at the level the user
   wants, which is often above where the client is today: a small perfumer is set beside the category's best, not
   beside other small shops.
2. **Open 3–5 of them** and capture each (`contact-sheet.cjs` for the page, `hero-frames.cjs` for the hero). Write what
   they actually do, as "Category look" in `PITCH.md`: type (size, case, weight, style), photography (subject, light,
   crop, how big on screen), background and colour, space and density, motion (pace, what moves), and copy voice
   (length, register). Name what they share and the one or two things that make them feel expensive or trusted. This is
   the brief: every builder gets it, and every variant matches its register.
   **Each one must pass "The bar" in `level.md`.** Drop any that don't (plain layouts, beige with a serif, small photos,
   cookie walls). If fewer than three pass, open the best of a neighbouring business that sells the same way or shares
   the customer's feeling (an emcee beside performers and personal brands, a clinic beside premium health and beauty),
   and write the Category look from the ones that pass.
3. **Facts versus target.** The client's own links and posts give the facts: products, prices, story, words to quote
   and the voice of the copy. They never set the look or the level. A casual Instagram doesn't make a casual site when
   the user or the category's best aim higher.
4. **The tone label is a hint.** Pick the nearest of the three below for motion and interaction levels and reference
   searches. When the label and the Category look disagree, the Category look wins.
5. **The level beats the Category look on how good.** The Level sites (`level.md`) set the type scale, how confident
   the colour is, how big and staged the subject is, and the space around it. The Category look sets what is shown,
   how formal it feels and the copy voice. Where they pull apart, the level wins.

- **Playful:** the business is about joy, treats, celebration, play or personality; customers come for fun or a feeling,
  whatever the price. Snacks, sweets, drinks, desserts, gifts, balloons and parties, toys and kids' things, pets, casual
  cafés and bars, playful fashion or lifestyle brands. **Looks like:** loud colour, bold type, stickers and cut-outs,
  bouncy motion, a hero action to play with, optional sound, copy with the brand's humour.
- **Modern and professional:** the business is about trust, performance or expertise; customers come for a result they
  must rely on: health, money, safety, learning, a business need, technology that has to work. Vehicles and technology,
  clinics and health, finance and law, education, B2B, property, services, group or corporate sites. **Looks like:** a
  calm, confident layout, clean type, precise motion that explains the product, real specs and proof up front; how
  light or dark, how large the type and how dense the page come from the business's character, not this label. No games, stickers, sound or jokes in headlines.
- **Luxury and premium** (fragrance and beauty, fashion, jewellery, watches, fine dining, boutique hotels, high-end
  interiors, heritage brands, or any client the user asks to look luxury or premium): calm like modern and professional,
  with art-directed imagery, a slower pace, far more space and type set the way the category's best set it.

Not by price: a cheap clinic visit is professional, an expensive party playful. Mixed signals: follow the Category look.

**Character: the direction in words.** Research writes 3–5 words for how this client should feel at the target level,
worked out from the Category look and the user's words, then checked against the client's facts so they stay true.
Never reuse words from another client. With the Category look, they set how every look decision feels (type case and
style, image treatment, contrast and colour, density, motion pace), always at the level's scale and confidence. Every
builder is briefed with both. Palettes stay
open to explore, light or dark, across the variants, inside the Category look's register.

**Check:** put the home page beside the Category look and Level sheets. If it wouldn't sit among them, or a stranger would
describe it with different words from the character, rework the look, not the idea.

**The direction decides the references.** Pick Benchmarks whose tone matches the hint, and put the Category look's
words in Awwwards searches (playful, colourful, illustrated; premium, minimal, technology, corporate; luxury, editorial,
fragrance). A reference in another register can't lead a variant, however good.

## Design families
Starting hints for categories that sell the same way, never a brief to copy: the Category look in `PITCH.md` wins. A
client that fits none takes the family matching how its page must sell.

Each family has a starting hero, built per "The hero" in `taste.md` from the client's real photos and footage: one stage,
one line of facts, one action, and something the visitor can try (playful) or a tool that answers a question
(professional). These are directions, not sites to copy: the concept in `PITCH.md` wins, and no two variants share a
hero.

- **One beautiful object:** bags, eyewear, watches, jewellery, shoes, perfume, audio, furniture. The product is the star:
  close-ups, materials, craft. **Hero:** the real product alone at the centre, big and sharp; scrolling moves into
  close-ups of materials and details, each part named.
- **Vehicles and mobility:** e-scooters, e-bikes, motorcycles, cars, EV charging, rental and sharing. Everyday technology,
  so precise and premium, never loud. **Hero:** the real vehicle, big and sharp in studio light or on a real street, or a
  real rider using it; it turns, folds or comes apart on scroll, parts named. A model switcher and one fact line (weight,
  range, price a month). Below: a feature index over close-ups, spec counters, "which model fits you", running cost
  against petrol. The brand's accent on a base the character suits, a clean grotesk. Never a game, a cartoon road, a
  dashboard or a sale poster. Category brands to open: cowboy.com, atherenergy.com, gogoro.com, unagiscooters.com.
- **Packs and jars:** snacks, drinks, sauces, coffee, sweets, pet food. Loud colour per product, flavours and ranges.
  **Hero:** the product being made or opened fills the screen, and the visitor does the last step (hold to fry, pour,
  crack the lid), ending in the basket. Each flavour comes out in its own shape and colour; small copy has the brand's
  humour.
- **Solves a problem:** skincare, hair, supplements, dental, pain relief, cleaning. Problem, proof, how it works.
  **Hero:** what the product does, under a headline in the customer's words. On scroll the problem clears (skin calms day
  by day, days counted), with how it works and whether it's safe (ingredients, tests, who can use it) on the way. Every
  claim, timeline and safety line comes from the client's material or is marked `[client to confirm]`. Below: explain it
  simply, with real before-and-after and simple diagrams, not paragraphs.
- **Body and movement:** physiotherapy, pilates, yoga, gyms, sports. The body in motion, not a calm studio. **Hero:** the
  client's real footage of a body in motion fills the screen, joints and muscles named as they work.
- **A place to visit:** restaurants, cafés, bars, hotels, spas, salons, gyms, clinics, photo studios. Sells the room and
  the visit. **Hero:** the real room or dish, sharp and whole at the centre, the effect around it (light, steam, the time
  of night). The cursor or a hold shows the next state: empty room to full, raw to plated, day to night. One line says
  what, where and when, next to the booking action. Room sound optional.
- **The person is the product:** emcees and hosts, event coordinators, DJs, musicians, speakers, coaches, consultants
  and personal brands. Customers book the person. **Hero:** the person staged like a product: one clean cut-out or a
  full-bleed portrait, big, in a decided colour, with their name or promise set huge around or behind them (Dennis
  Snellenberg, Ario Law, Charles Leclerc in `level.md`). Event photos come below as proof, never a grid in the hero.
  Weak photos: a short shoot list for one studio portrait session goes in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`, and meanwhile the best
  single portrait, cut out clean.
- **Work worth showing:** event styling, florists, weddings, interiors, architecture, renovation, fit-out,
  design-and-build and contractors, photography, tattoo, set design. The portfolio is the proof. **Hero:** before and
  after in one move: the bare space, filled with the finished work by the visitor's hold or cursor. Below: job names,
  sized to the character, whose hover opens the work, and one line or thread carrying you through the page. For spaces (interiors,
  architecture, renovation, fit-out): generous space around the work, photos framed and shown in their own light, never
  dimmed to carry text, and the trade's own drawing language as the detail layer: floor plans, dimension lines, drawing
  numbers and title blocks, "Detail 1" callouts on photos, "not to scale" notes.
- **Guides, media and creators:** city guides, places-to-go and review accounts, listings and directories, event
  calendars, creator channels and communities. They sell a visit or outing, not a product: places, prices and where to
  go, with the creator's own voice and posts as proof. **Hero:** the best places or episodes as real photos or footage,
  each with its area, stepping through on scroll, with one action such as "see all spots" or "plan my weekend". Below:
  filters by area, type and price, a map, the latest posts, how they visit and film. Category brands to open:
  timeout.com, theinfatuation.com, atlasobscura.com, airbnb.com/s/experiences.
- **Trust and expertise:** finance, law, accounting, insurance, consultants, B2B suppliers. Calm, clear, credible.
  **Hero:** what the client really delivers, in real photos or footage: the product, or the people at work and the
  customer's result (the finished building, the case won, the money saved). A slider or calculator answers the money
  question early. No sound, games, props or rituals; plain words; slower motion.
- **Learning and community:** courses, coaching, academies, mentorships, schools. A person, a method, student results.
  **Hero:** the teacher and real students at work, or a real result they reached, the promise in one line and a first
  step you can try. Proof stays labelled; risk wording stays.
- **Origin and craft:** farms, wineries, distilleries, makers, heritage brands. A place and how it's made. **Hero:** the
  finished product big at the centre, with real photos of the place and the making around it; on scroll a route draws
  from the source to the product, each stop opening a chapter.
